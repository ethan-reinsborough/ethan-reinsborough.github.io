§0 · ORIENTATION

How to read this doc

If you are new to the team, read sections 1–4 first (what the game is, who it's for, what's sacred). Sections 5–8 are the working reference: roadmap, monetization, the Cards feature, and the technical architecture. Section 9 is how we work together day to day.

The brand voice in this doc is dialed down from how we talk to players — formal enough to be a reference, but you should still leave understanding what makes Bloxodachi feel like Bloxodachi, not just what it contains.

A note on naming: in code and in anything a player reads, the characters are always “Robloxians.” Never “residents,” “Miis,” or “NPCs.” This doc uses “Robloxians” the same way. It's a small rule that protects the brand from looking like a Tomodachi clone.

§1 · THE GAME

What Bloxodachi Life is

A god-view life simulator on Roblox, inspired by Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream. The player is not an avatar in the world — they're an unseen presence above it, watching their island of Robloxians live, talk, eat, dream, form relationships, and grow. You add Robloxians, customize them from the Roblox catalog, give them voices and personalities, then nudge their lives along: feed them, answer their questions, drag two of them together to spark a friendship, watch a friendship turn into a romance and a romance turn into a wedding.

It is cozy (low-stakes, no fail states, no timers breathing down your neck) and it is chaotic (Robloxians get hiccups, fall over, develop crushes you didn't plan, stand unbothered in front of hyper-realistic PNG backgrounds). That tension is the whole point.

Built solo on a Claude Opus + Codex stack. Organic growth — TikTok virality, word of mouth, and the Roblox algorithm surfacing the game off elite retention. This doc marks the transition from solo development to team-supported scaling.

The market gap it fills

Tomodachi-style life simulation had no major presence on Roblox, despite two proven adjacent genres: avatar-customization games regularly hit 50K–100K+ CCU, and life-sim games (Brookhaven, Adopt Me, Bloxburg) clear 100K+. Bloxodachi sits at the intersection — avatar customization plus life simulation, with social storytelling as the differentiating layer. Nobody else is doing the Tomodachi relationship system at scale on the platform.

§2 · WHO IT'S FOR

Target audience

🎨 Primary

Roblox players roughly 10–18, with artistic and social interests. They make fanart, care about character expression, live in creative communities, and share content. TikTok has trained them for short loops — but they go deep once a game earns it.

🌱 Secondary

Players 18–30 nostalgic for Tomodachi Life, Animal Crossing, and Chao Garden. Adults who want cozy gameplay with real depth.

The read that matters

This audience is not “less capable” or “shorter attention.” They are more selective about what earns engagement, and they engage harder than older audiences once hooked.

Design implication: build deep features with smart onboarding. Shallow features built for an assumed short attention span will underperform. But — and this is the counterweight — a simple feature with a real hook is often a huge win. Pets and the potion shop were both fast to build and both landed hard. Depth and simplicity aren't opposites here; the bar is “is there a real hook,” not “is it complicated.”

§3 · THE FIVE

Design pillars

Every feature decision gets evaluated against these five. If a feature doesn't serve at least one, question it.

🌼

Cozy Chaos

Peaceful, low-stakes interaction balanced against absurd emergent moments. The player should feel safe and unhurried while regularly being surprised and delighted.

💞

Meaningful Relationships Over Competition

The core is social simulation. Players invest in their Robloxians' relationships and stories. The Social Hub adds competition, but the heart is emotional attachment, not winning.

🧑‍🎨

Customization Through the Roblox Catalog

Players express identity through the full catalog character creator. Robloxians are recognizably Roblox-native — deliberately not the Mii aesthetic.

📸

Sharable Absurdity

Built for the TikTok era. The Charm Contest, World Tour PNG backgrounds, resident chaos — these manufacture screenshot- and clip-worthy moments. Virality is baked in, not bolted on.

💝

Voluntary Support, Not Pay-to-Win

Monetization frames spending as supporting the game and unlocking expression — never as buying competitive advantage. (Revisitable for multiplayer specifically, where the math changes.)

§6.1 · How “never the only path” holds — a worked example

A romance system that also sells a Love Potion invites an obvious worry: does spending quietly become the real path to marriage? It doesn't, and the code is deliberately built to keep it that way.

Robloxians fall in love for free. The CrushSpark event is a thought bubble that fires organically (~3% of special-event rolls, tuned up so it's discoverable) with no relationship-stage gate — any mutually-interested pair can spark, even strangers. The player taps “It might be. Go talk to them,” and the romance begins; milestone cutscenes (Beach Run → Stargaze → Proposal → Wedding) carry the pair to marriage.

The Love Potion accelerates, it never gates. It advances an eligible pair one romantic stage and plays that stage's cutscene — a player who never spends still reaches a wedding by playing. That ordering — free path first, paid acceleration second — is the rule every future monetized system should follow.

§6.2 · Singleplayer-first

The original vision included multiplayer: an 8-player split-island scheme with spectate-only access to other islands. We deliberately shipped singleplayer-first — not as a permanent constraint, but as sequencing. The core life-sim loop has to be genuinely fun for one player before a shared world is worth the complexity.

“Singleplayer” describes the island sim, not the whole game. Cross-player social is already arriving through the Social Hub and Bloxodachi Cards (§7).

§6.3 · The north star — fanfic scenes

Why it's the right north star:

  • It's the in-engine version of what the audience already does (fanart, fanfic). A scene authoring tool is that impulse made native.
  • It's maximally shareable — pairs naturally with Cards (§7).
  • The hard infrastructure is already built. We're maturing an engine, not inventing one.

Treat as destination, not backlog. Every improvement to poses, camera presets, and the beat runner is an investment in this endgame.

§7 · FLAGSHIP FEATURE · pre-build

Bloxodachi Cards / Pages

The “turn attachment into portable identity” feature. A player takes a Robloxian they love, generates a card for them in-game, and that card becomes a public, shareable object — a profile page on the web, a card a Discord bot can render, a thing you post.

Why it fits the pillars

  • Sharable Absurdity: a card is the most natively-shareable artifact the game can produce.
  • Meaningful Relationships: emotional attachment extends past the session — your Robloxian exists outside the game now.
  • Customization: another expression surface; pulls identity value out of catalog work the player already did.

Phased build

MVP

📸 Photo Booth + backend + pages

  • In-game Photo Booth — pick, pose, frame, generate.
  • Reuses god-view camera + ViewportFrame portrait infra.
  • Backend storage keyed to player + Robloxian (external service from the start).
  • Public profile pages — shareable by link, screenshot-friendly.
Phase 2

🤖 Discord bot integration

  • Bot renders cards in Discord.
  • Supports collection / trading-style social mechanics.
  • Reads from the same backend the web pages do.
Phase 3

🔁 Game ↔ web ↔ game feedback loop

  • Web / Discord actions feed back into the game.
  • Candidates: redeemables, web-side customization that appears in-game, social signals surfaced on the island.
  • Exact mechanics TBD.

§8 · PHILOSOPHY

Monetization philosophy

The rule

Monetization follows attachment. It never precedes it. Build the engagement loop, prove players want in, then introduce optional premium layers. This is not a moral position alone — it's what the data has rewarded.

✅ What's working

  • Founders Pass — recognition-based; players supporting the game's success.
  • Potions / items — functional but optional; enhance expression, never gate progression.
  • Milestone promo codes — build goodwill, reward the engaged community.

Principles

  • Never gate core gameplay behind a paywall.
  • Limited-time items create urgency without forcing an immediate buy.
  • Don't fake urgency. A permanent Game Pass should not wear a “LIMITED TIME” badge — we removed one for exactly this reason.
  • Premium items should feel like collectibles or status symbols, not power upgrades.
  • Discounted “founders” / “milestone” packs at significant moments work well.
  • Free rewards for everyone, alongside premium options, maintains goodwill.

A worked example in code

When every Robloxian slot is full, the game shows a soft “Island full — tap to make more room” nudge that opens the Heart Shop, hidden for players who already own the More Friends Pass. The instinct in a lesser game would be to nerf progression to manufacture that pressure — which would punish the most engaged players, who are exactly the monetization funnel. Instead we convert the natural friction point and leave progression generous.

That's the philosophy made concrete: pressure comes from real ceilings, never from artificial pain.

Future opportunities (not now): seasonal card frames (§7.4), premium card effects, earnable + premium housing decoration, limited Wandering Merchant exclusives, Discord bot cosmetics.

§9 — Technical architecture overview

Non-tech readers: this section is the orientation layer for engineers — system shape, code paths, and the gotchas that have actually bitten us. You can skip it without missing any product context.

Full detail lives in the codebase, docs/SYSTEM_MAP.md, and docs/COLLAB.md. New engineers: read SYSTEM_MAP and COLLAB before touching the relationship, stats, or cutscene systems — they have hard-won invariants that are easy to violate.

9.1 — Shape of the project

  • Roblox + Rojo. Code lives in this repo and syncs into Studio. *.server.lua → Script, *.client.lua → LocalScript, plain *.lua → ModuleScript. Tabs for indentation.
  • Service properties belong in default.project.json $properties, not scripts. Setting Workspace.StreamingEnabled or Lighting.Technology from a script throws a capability error and halts the script. Single most common environment trap.
AreaPathHolds
Shared modulessrc/ReplicatedStorage/Shared/GameConfig, Relationships, Personalities, Activities, RobloxianEvents, Items, Treasure, Potions, Pets, BloxodachiDialogue, CutsceneEngine, ScriptedPoses, DulcetVoice…
Serversrc/ServerScriptService/Authority. Bootstrap, Services/*, behavior scripts (RobloxianIdle/Inspect/Drag/Gifting), Cutscenes/* scene definitions.
Clientsrc/StarterPlayer/StarterPlayerScripts/All UI and presentation: Camera, MobileJoystick, HUD, inspect/creation UIs, RelationshipUI, CutsceneClient, speech, lighting tweens, shop UIs.

9.2 — The architectural spine

God-view, no character

Players.CharacterAutoLoads = false. Camera in Camera.client.lua is fully scriptable: angled top-down, WASD/edge pan, scroll zoom, RMB rotate, clamped to the island. Because there is no character, the mobile joystick is custom — Roblox's native DynamicThumbstick is character-control UI and won't render reliably here. (Player-tested decision: joystick is deliberately the default; earlier swipe-to-pan primary was found “way too hard.” Fix bugs; don't re-propose swipe-primary.)

The world rebuilds on every server start

IslandWorld.Build() destroys and recreates Workspace.BloxodachiIsland. Do not add persistence to the world folder. Island can be generated or authored (MainIsland / SakuraIsland); authored islands are detected and preserved, system reads EventMarker… parts for activity locations.

Server is the only writer of state

Every Robloxian's stats live in profile.RuntimeState server-side. Clients render from remote payloads and never mutate authoritative state. Critical invariant: if a remote claims to own multiple slices of state, it must populate all of them — or send a deliberately partial payload that the client's nil-guards skip. A stale full snapshot wipes freshly-displayed values.

Persistence is centralized in SessionPersistenceService

Robloxian profiles, player inventory, purchases, Word Bank, island level/progress, unlock claims all export/import through it. Shops don't persist stock (deterministic from UTC day index). Studio runs with persistence off (writes to _Studio suffix); live servers always persist.

9.3 — Key gameplay systems (one line each)

  • Robloxians — forced R15 for reliable catalog animation; intentionally oversized (~1.75×). Appearance mixes HumanoidDescription fields with a generated face-feature SurfaceGui.
  • Voice — animalese-style letter chirps (DulcetVoice); all speech flows through one RobloxianSpoke event.
  • Stats & mood — Hunger drains slowly. Happiness is the visible level meter — no separate EXP. Raising Happiness grants equal Coins through the single AwardHappiness path.
  • Activities — weighted scheduler; solo + multi-actor. Background activities are flavor only — they never award relationship points.
  • Relationships — 10+ stage ladder in Relationships.lua, data-only so stages insert cleanly. Progression is player-engaged only.
  • Events / bubbles — head bubbles are “something's on my mind” indicators; engagement happens via normal click flow.
  • Cutscenes — data-driven beat runner (CutsceneEngine + Cutscenes/*). Animations run client-side (server LoadAnimation doesn't replicate reliably for non-character models); rig-stability fix is anchoring only the HumanoidRootPart.
  • Monetization plumbing — GameConfig.Monetization.Products, MarketplaceReceipts for ProcessReceipt (saves before acknowledging), Heart Shop, “Coming Soon” disabled state for any product whose dashboard ID isn't pasted yet.

9.4 — Things that will bite you

Don't chase Robloxian head-lag / walking choppiness for V1

Deeply investigated. Root cause is live-humanoid CFrame replication with no client interpolation, plus catalog-heavy avatars. SLIM/streaming was tested and made it worse; RenderFidelity is plugin-locked. Shipped mitigation is camera-aware visual LOD. Real fix is client-side animation/interpolation — a project, not a patch.

Don't tween lighting server-side

Clients won't see smooth interpolation. Day/night is split: DayNightService owns Lighting.ClockTime, DayNightLighting.client.lua owns all visual tweens. God-view makes OutdoorAmbient and ExposureCompensation matter more than usual.

User text must pass TextModerationService

Before it's saved or shown to another player. Applies to Robloxian names, Word Bank entries, and anything new that's player-authored (island names, captions, card text).

The Clothes Shop is being retired

Being repurposed (likely as the Potion Stand). Don't wire new monetization to VipClothingService / Items.VipCatalog. Codex currently owns the creator rework (RobloxianCreation.*) — coordinate before touching it.

§10 · WORKFLOW

How we work

How to work effectively with the founder and alongside the AI dev stack.

§10.1 · Decision-making operating system

The founder's system that built this game: Trend Recognition + Execution Speed + Iteration Off Analytics. Run decisions through five questions:

  1. What is the data telling us? Retention, CCU, monetization, ratings.
  2. What is the community asking for? Discord, in-game behavior.
  3. Does this serve the design pillars? (See §3.)
  4. Can we ship a meaningful version quickly?
  5. Does this open new content runway, or close it?

When in doubt: ship fast and iterate. The Bloxodachi advantage is execution velocity, not first-pass polish. Polish comes from iterating on real player response.

Resist: scope creep · perfection-before-shipping · ignoring data because the team has opinions · building for hypothetical players instead of actual ones.

§10.2 · Decision authority & creative direction flow

  • Creative direction and the “sacred” list (§4) flow from the founder. When in doubt about anything sacred, ask before building.
  • Within the flexible list, contributors have real latitude — bring a working slice, not a proposal deck.
  • New features that aren't on the plan get checked in first. If you think something belongs on the roadmap, add it to docs/PLAN.md and flag it — don't silently build it.
  • The founder favors lean process. Name the cost of overhead up front; default to the lightest workflow that works. Heavy collaboration ceremony is friction, not safety.

§10.3 · Brand voice guardrails

The voice is a real brand asset. All player-facing text — in-game strings, announcements, marketing, social, descriptions — follows it.

Tonal principles: slightly chaotic but never mean · affectionate sarcasm toward Robloxians, never toward players · self-aware about absurdity without explaining it · warm and inclusive without being saccharine.

Hard rules: never explain a joke · never apologize for absurdity · reference established lore as community in-jokes (the missing llamas, the router from the sky, the unbothered Robloxians in front of PNG backgrounds) · lowercase is fine in casual social contexts.

Calibration samples

your island is about to be a full soap opera and i will not be apologizing

Note: in community copy the characters are often called “residents” for vibe; in game UI and code they are always “Robloxians.” Keep that line.

§10.4 · Working in the codebase — for engineers / AI agents

  • Read AGENTS.md, docs/PLAN.md, docs/COLLAB.md, and recent git log before starting.
  • Git is the change history. COLLAB.md is reserved for non-obvious gotchas and sticky decisions only.
  • Land working slices. If you stop mid-feature, commit what works.
  • No speculative abstractions, no backwards-compat shims. Greenfield — delete unused code, don't comment it out.
  • Multi-agent repo (Claude + Codex). Check live working sets before editing a file another agent owns.
